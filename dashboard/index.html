Central load × attention networks

Reanalysis of the NJP central-load fMRI study: 8 subjects, about 155 runs. A central red/blue majority-color judgment gets harder as the minority proportion rises from 10% to 46%. A task-irrelevant 1/f noise annulus fills the periphery (3–7.5°). Betas come from the original per-subject FS-FAST fsaverage GLMs; ROIs come from the Wang 2015, Glasser 2016 and Schaefer 2018 atlases.

Caveats & provenance

  • Load is confounded with run position. Each run has a single ratio, and each subject's ratio order repeats one shuffle, so slow drift within a session could look like a load effect. The existing GLMs can't separate these. Fixing it needs a refit with run position as a covariate.
  • S12 and S13 stay at about 80% correct at every ratio, even after the key-reversal correction. Use the subject filter to check that the results hold without them.
  • Foveal increases could partly reflect stimulus or motor differences between ratios (the red/blue mix, the number of responses), not attention alone.
  • Eccentricity bands come from the Benson 2014 template, not from subject pRFs. Wang IPS4 and IPS5 are merged because IPS5 is only 18–53 vertices.
  • Units: FS-FAST betas after intensity normalization to a mean of 100, so they are roughly % signal change relative to fixation.
  • Checks: the CAM.par conditions match the behavioral ratio in of runs. The Wang label order was checked against fsaverage anatomy and its overlap with Benson V1–V3. Code is in scripts/, and the SCC pipeline is described in the README.
